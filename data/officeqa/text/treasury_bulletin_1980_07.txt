Background
Data

Reports cover nine major foreign exchange market currencies
and
United

Reporting

Statistics

,

"Majority-owned

"Majority-owned

of
all

to
vote,
or

than
50
percent
of
the
total value of all classes
of stock.
The

as

1,
1978,
for
the
weekly
reports,
and
as
of
October
31,
1978
(the
last
business
day
of
the month),
for
the monthly reports.
The most recent revision of the nonbank
foreign currency
forms
(see

became
effective
as

the
last business day of September 1978.
Among
the changes
on

Common Definitions and Concepts
The term "United States" means the States of the United
States,
the District of Columbia,
the Commonwealth of Puerto
Rico,
American
Samoa,

The

"foreign" means

other
than
the
"United
States",
The

"worldwide"

the sum of
"United
States"
and "foreign"

Data

the United States include amounts reported by

partnerships,
and corporations
in
the
United

including

Data

"foreign

"abroad"

In general,

"Short-term"

The currencies

ally

to
the collecting agencies
to
defray

subject

the regular appro-
priation processes.
The act
of July
15,
1952,
Sec.
1415
(66
Stat. 662),
pro-
vided
that after June
30,
1953,
U.S.
agencies could no longer
expend foreign currencies belonging
to
the
U. S* Government
except as provided annually
in appropriation acts.
Subsequent
legislation required executive departments and agencies, with
certain exceptions,
to reimburse
the Treasury
in dollars for
the foreign currencies used.
Executive Orders
No. 10488 dated
September
23,
1953,
and
No.
10900 dated January
5,
1961,
as
amended,
provided
for
the
issuance of
regulations by
the Sec-
retary of
the Treasury governing
the purchase,
custody,
de-
posit,
transfer,
and

of foreign exchange by
all Execu-
tive Departments and agencies
of
the
U.S.
Government.
Accordingly,

No.
930
was
issued on
October
19,
1953,
effective December
1,
1953.
These regu-
lations provided
for
