From August 12 through August 13, 2025, a representative of the Pipeline and Hazardous
Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA), Office of Pipeline Safety (OPS), pursuant to Chapter
601 of 49 United States Code (U.S.C.), inspected ANR Pipeline Company’s (ANR) Muttonville
underground natural gas storage facility (UNGSF) in Troy, Michigan. 1
As a result of the inspection, it is alleged that ANR has committed a probable violation of the
Pipeline Safety Regulations, Title 49, Code of Federal Regulations (CFR). The item inspected and
the probable violation is:
1.

§ 192.12 Underground natural gas storage facilities.
(a) …
(b) Depleted hydrocarbon and aquifer reservoir UNGSFs.
(1) …

(2) Each UNGSF that uses a depleted hydrocarbon reservoir or an
aquifer reservoir for natural gas storage and was constructed on or
before July 18, 2017, must meet the provisions of API RP 1171
(incorporated by reference, see § 192.7), sections 8, 9, 10, and 11, and
paragraph (c) of this section, by January 18, 2018, and must meet all
provisions of paragraph (d) of this section by March 13, 2021.
ANR failed to meet the provisions of API RP 1171 section 9 in accordance with section
192.12(b)(2). Specifically, for all 17 wells at the Muttonville UNGSF, ANR failed to perform
annual master valve function tests from 2023 through 2025 and annual master valve isolation tests
from 2021 through 2025 in accordance with section 9.3.2 in API RP 1171.
Section 9.3.2 states in part that “[t]he operator shall test the operation of the master valve and
wellhead pipeline isolation valve at least annually for proper function and ability to isolate the
well.”
In addition, ANR’s procedures, including the TC Energy Operations and Maintenance ManualU.S. Natural Gas Pipelines and Underground Natural Gas Storage Facilities, Rev. 40
(11/03/2025) (O&M), Table 192.12, section 9.3.2 stated that the “operator shall test the operation
of the master valve and wellhead pipeline isolation valve at least annually for proper function and
ability to isolate the well.”
During the inspection, PHMSA reviewed ANR’s records of wellhead valve testing. The
Muttonville Master Valve Data Spreadsheet for 2021-2025 (Records) indicated that ANR failed
to perform annual master valve function tests from 2023 through 2025 and annual master valve
isolation tests from 2021 through 2025 for all 17 wells due to subsurface safety valve tubing
present through the master valve. In the Records for the 2021 attempted master valve isolation
testing, ANR admitted that it could not test the master valve noting that it “[c]an’t close wellhead
valve due to safety valve tubing.” While the Records demonstrated that ANR tested other valves
(such as the subsurface safety valve or pipeline isolation valve), these tests do not replace or satisfy
the section 9.3.2 requirement to function test and verify the isolation capability of the master valve.
Therefore, ANR failed to meet the provisions of API RP 1171 section 9 in accordance with section
192.12(b)(2).

PROPOSED COMPLIANCE ORDER
Pursuant to 49 United States Code § 60118, the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety
Administration (PHMSA) proposes to issue to ANR Pipeline Company (ANR) a Compliance
Order incorporating the following remedial requirements to ensure the compliance with the
pipeline safety regulations:
A.

In regard to Item 1 of the Notice, ANR must, for the 17 wellheads at Muttonville
underground natural gas storage facility, test the operation of the master valves for
proper function and ability to isolate the well and provide records of the testing to
the Director, Eastern Region, within 365 days of receipt of the Final Order.

B.

It is requested (not mandated) that ANR Pipeline Company maintain
documentation of the safety improvement costs associated with fulfilling this
Compliance Order and submit the total to Robert Burrough, Director, Eastern
Region, Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration. It is requested
that these costs be reported in two categories: 1) total cost associated with
preparation/revision of plans, procedures, studies and analyses, and 2) total cost
associated with replacements, additions and other changes to pipeline
infrastructure.
