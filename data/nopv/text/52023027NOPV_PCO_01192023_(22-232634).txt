From June 7 through 8, 2022, a representative of the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety
Administration (PHMSA), pursuant to Chapter 601of 49 United States Code (U.S.C.), inspected the
facilities and records of NAFTEX Operating Company’s (Naftex) gas transmission pipeline in
Bakersfield, California.
As a result of the inspection, it is alleged that you have committed a probable violation of the
Pipeline Safety Regulations, Title 49, Code of Federal Regulations (CFR). The item inspected, and
the probable violation is:
1.

§ 192.605 Procedural manual for operations, maintenance, and emergencies.
(a) General. Each operator shall prepare and follow for each pipeline, a manual of
written procedures for conducting operations and maintenance activities and for
emergency response. For transmission lines, the manual must also include procedures
for handling abnormal operations. This manual must be reviewed and updated by the
operator at intervals not exceeding 15 months, but at least once each calendar year.
This manual must be prepared before operations of a pipeline system commence.

Appropriate parts of the manual must be kept at locations where operations and
maintenance activities are conducted.
Naftex failed to follow for each pipeline, a manual of written procedures for conducting operations
and maintenance activities and for emergency response. Specifically, Naftex failed to follow its Gas
Pipeline Operations & Maintenance Manual, Procedure 6.01-Atmospheric Corrosion. Section 5.1 of
the procedure requires that Naftex inspect all above-ground pipe for atmospheric corrosion every 3
calendar years, not to exceed 39 months, and section 5.7 of the procedure requires that Naftex
complete any repairs or maintenance actions resulting from the atmospheric corrosion inspection
before the next atmospheric corrosion inspection. 1 During the field inspection, PHMSA observed
and photographed the above-ground pipe inside the fence and on the upstream side of the fenced area
at the Mojave interconnect, showing severe atmospheric corrosion and unprotected bare pipe. 2 After
the inspection, Naftex provided PHMSA with a copy of the three most recent atmospheric corrosion
inspection reports. When reviewing the reports, PHMSA found that the above-ground pipe inside the
fence and on the upstream side of the fenced area were not inspected during their 2019 and 2021
atmospheric corrosion inspections. 3 Based on the severity of the atmospheric corrosion observed by
PHMSA, it is likely that some atmospheric corrosion existed at the time of the 2019 atmospheric
corrosion inspection, and if it had been included on the report, it should have been corrected by the
2021 atmospheric corrosion inspection in accordance with section 5.7 of the procedure.
Proposed Compliance Order

We have reviewed the circumstances and supporting documents involved in this case and have
decided not to propose a civil penalty assessment at this time.

PROPOSED COMPLIANCE ORDER
Pursuant to 49 U.S.C. § 60118, the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration
(PHMSA) proposes to issue to NAFTEX Operating Company (Naftex) a Compliance Order
incorporating the following remedial requirements to ensure the compliance of Naftex with the
pipeline safety regulations:
A.

In regard to Item 1 of the Notice pertaining to the failure to follow for each pipeline, a
manual of written procedures for conducting operations and maintenance activities
and for emergency response, within 90 days of receipt of the Final Order, Naftex
must:
1. Clean and coat the above-ground pipe inside the fence and on the upstream
side of the fenced area; and
2. Submit photographs to PHMSA to show that above-ground pipe inside the
fence and on the upstream side of the fenced area has been cleaned and
coated.

B.

It is requested (not mandated) that Naftex maintain documentation of the safety
improvement costs associated with fulfilling this Compliance Order and submit the
total to Dustin Hubbard, Director, Western Region, Pipeline and Hazardous Materials
Safety Administration. It is requested that these costs be reported in two categories:
1) total cost associated with preparation/revision of plans, procedures, studies, and
analyses, and 2) total cost associated with replacements, additions, and other changes
to pipeline infrastructure.
